Question 1 (10 points)
Question: According to the passage, what are the main benefits that technology has brought to modern communication? Provide at least three specific examples from the text.

EXPECTED ANSWER:
Students should identify and explain at least three benefits from the passage:

1. Real-time global connectivity - Technology enables people to communicate across continents instantly, maintaining relationships despite physical distance.

2. Multimedia sharing - People can share experiences through various forms of content (photos, videos, messages), enriching communication beyond just text.

3. Democratized access to information - Technology has made information and knowledge accessible to people who previously lacked such opportunities.

4. Online education platforms - Digital tools have made learning accessible to millions, breaking down traditional barriers to education.

5. Creative expression and community building - Technology empowers individuals to create and share content, forming communities based on shared interests rather than just geography.

GRADING CRITERIA:
- Full credit (10 points): Identifies 3+ benefits with clear explanations and specific references to the text
- Good (7-9 points): Identifies 3 benefits but explanations lack detail or specificity
- Satisfactory (5-6 points): Identifies 2 benefits with reasonable explanation
- Needs improvement (3-4 points): Identifies only 1-2 benefits with minimal explanation
- Insufficient (0-2 points): Vague or incorrect responses

KEY CONCEPTS: connectivity, multimedia, democratization, education, creative expression, community

================================================================================

Question 2 (10 points)
Question: Explain the concept of "echo chambers" as described in the passage. How do social media algorithms contribute to this phenomenon, and what are the potential consequences?

EXPECTED ANSWER:
Students should demonstrate understanding of:

1. Echo Chamber Definition - A situation where users primarily encounter information that confirms their existing beliefs, limiting exposure to diverse perspectives.

2. Algorithm's Role - Social media algorithms prioritize engagement over accuracy, showing users content similar to what they've previously engaged with, reinforcing existing viewpoints.

3. Consequences - This leads to:
   - Polarization (people becoming more extreme in their views)
   - Reduced exposure to diverse perspectives
   - Amplification of misinformation and sensational content
   - Confirmation bias (seeking information that confirms pre-existing beliefs)

GRADING CRITERIA:
- Full credit (10 points): Clear explanation of echo chambers, algorithm role, and multiple consequences with good understanding
- Good (7-9 points): Explains concept and consequences but may miss some details about algorithms
- Satisfactory (5-6 points): Basic understanding but incomplete explanation
- Needs improvement (3-4 points): Partial understanding with significant gaps
- Insufficient (0-2 points): Misunderstands the concept or provides irrelevant information

KEY CONCEPTS: echo chambers, algorithms, confirmation bias, polarization, misinformation, engagement metrics

================================================================================

Question 3 (10 points)
Question: The passage mentions that technology has "blurred the boundaries between work and personal life." Discuss what this means and explain the negative effects this can have on individuals.

EXPECTED ANSWER:
Students should explain:

1. Meaning of Blurred Boundaries - Constant availability through mobile devices means people can be reached for work at any time, making it difficult to separate work hours from personal time. The distinction between "at work" and "off work" becomes unclear.

2. Negative Effects - The passage specifically mentions:
   - Increased stress
   - Burnout
   
   Students may also reasonably infer:
   - Difficulty relaxing or disconnecting
   - Impact on work-life balance
   - Reduced quality time with family/friends
   - Mental health challenges

GRADING CRITERIA:
- Full credit (10 points): Clear explanation of blurred boundaries with specific mention of stress and burnout, plus thoughtful discussion
- Good (7-9 points): Explains the concept and mentions negative effects from the passage
- Satisfactory (5-6 points): Basic understanding but lacks depth or misses key effects
- Needs improvement (3-4 points): Vague explanation or misses main points
- Insufficient (0-2 points): Does not understand the concept

KEY CONCEPTS: work-life balance, constant availability, stress, burnout, boundaries, mobile devices

Question 4 (10 points)
Question: The author suggests that the key question is "how to harness technology's benefits while mitigating its negative effects." Based on the passage and your own understanding, propose two specific strategies that individuals or society could implement to achieve this balance.

EXPECTED ANSWER:
This is an open-ended question requiring critical thinking. Students should propose reasonable, specific strategies. Examples of good responses:

INDIVIDUAL STRATEGIES:
- Developing digital literacy skills (mentioned in passage)
- Establishing healthy boundaries with technology use (mentioned in passage)
- Setting specific times for checking devices/social media
- Practicing face-to-face communication intentionally
- Being critical consumers of online information
- Taking regular digital detoxes

SOCIETAL STRATEGIES:
- Designing platforms that prioritize human well-being over engagement (mentioned in passage)
- Teaching digital literacy in schools
- Regulating social media algorithms to reduce echo chambers
- Promoting diverse perspectives in online spaces
- Creating workplace policies about after-hours communication

GRADING CRITERIA:
- Full credit (10 points): Two specific, well-reasoned strategies with clear explanations of how they address the problem
- Good (7-9 points): Two strategies that are reasonable but may lack specificity or depth
- Satisfactory (5-6 points): One good strategy or two vague strategies
- Needs improvement (3-4 points): Strategies are too general or not well-explained
- Insufficient (0-2 points): No clear strategies or irrelevant responses

KEY CONCEPTS: digital literacy, boundaries, platform design, well-being, balance, regulation, education

================================================================================

Question 5 (10 points)
Question: Do you agree with the passage's claim that face-to-face communication skills are declining due to technology? Support your position with reasoning and examples from your own observations or experiences.

EXPECTED ANSWER:
This is a personal reflection question. Students can agree or disagree, but must support their position with reasoning and examples.

IF AGREEING:
- Should reference the passage's point about loss of body language, tone, and emotional expression
- Should provide personal observations or examples
- Should show understanding of what face-to-face communication entails
- May discuss younger generations growing up with devices

IF DISAGREEING:
- Should acknowledge the passage's concerns but provide counterarguments
- Should provide evidence or examples of maintained communication skills
- May argue that technology creates different, not inferior, communication skills
- Should still demonstrate understanding of the passage's argument

GRADING CRITERIA:
- Full credit (10 points): Clear position with well-reasoned arguments and specific examples; shows critical thinking
- Good (7-9 points): Clear position with reasoning but examples may be limited or less specific
- Satisfactory (5-6 points): States position but reasoning is basic or examples are weak
- Needs improvement (3-4 points): Position unclear or poorly supported
- Insufficient (0-2 points): No clear position or irrelevant response

KEY CONCEPTS: face-to-face communication, body language, tone, emotional expression, personal experience, observations, reasoning, examples, critical thinking

GENERAL GRADING NOTES:

1. Partial Credit: Award partial credit for partially correct answers that show some understanding.

2. Writing Quality: While this is primarily a content assessment, extremely poor writing that obscures meaning may result in minor deductions.

3. Text References: Students should demonstrate they've read and understood the passage by referencing specific points.

4. Critical Thinking: Questions 4 and 5 assess higher-order thinking. Look for evidence of analysis, synthesis, and evaluation, not just recall.

5. Originality: For reflection questions, value original thinking and personal insights, even if they differ from expected answers, as long as they're well-reasoned.
