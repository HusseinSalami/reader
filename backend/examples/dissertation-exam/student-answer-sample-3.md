### Question 2 (10 points)
**Question:** Explain the concept of "echo chambers" as described in the passage. How do social media algorithms contribute to this phenomenon, and what are the potential consequences?

**Your Answer:**
Echo chambers are when people only see information that confirms what they already believe. The passage says social media algorithms make this happen by prioritizing engagement instead of accuracy. The algorithms show you more of what you've liked or engaged with before, so you keep seeing similar content and opinions. This creates confirmation bias. The consequences include polarization (people becoming more extreme), less exposure to different perspectives, and spreading of misinformation and sensational content. It's a problem because people need to hear different viewpoints to make informed decisions, but the algorithms keep them in bubbles where everyone thinks the same way.

### Question 3 (10 points)
**Question:** The passage mentions that technology has "blurred the boundaries between work and personal life." Discuss what this means and explain the negative effects this can have on individuals.

**Your Answer:**
This means that work and personal time are no longer separate because mobile devices let people be contacted anytime. Before technology, when you left work you were done for the day, but now your boss or colleagues can reach you at night, on weekends, or during vacation. The passage says this causes increased stress and burnout. These negative effects happen because people can't properly rest and recharge. If you're always thinking about work or responding to work messages, you never fully relax. This can damage your mental health, hurt your relationships with family and friends, and eventually lead to burnout where you're exhausted and can't function well at work or in personal life.

**Your Answer:**
First strategy: Teach digital literacy in schools as a core subject. The passage mentions developing digital literacy skills as important. Students should learn how to fact-check information, understand how algorithms work, recognize echo chambers, and use technology in healthy ways. Second strategy: Set clear boundaries for technology use in personal life. The passage talks about establishing healthy boundaries. This could mean rules like no phones during family meals, turning off work notifications after 6pm, having phone-free bedrooms to improve sleep, and scheduling regular time for face-to-face interactions with friends and family. These boundaries would help people maintain work-life balance and preserve important in-person communication skills.
