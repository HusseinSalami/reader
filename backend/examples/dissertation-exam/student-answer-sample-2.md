### Question 2 (10 points)
**Question:** Explain the concept of "echo chambers" as described in the passage. How do social media algorithms contribute to this phenomenon, and what are the potential consequences?

**Your Answer:**
Echo chambers are environments where users mainly encounter information that confirms their existing beliefs. Social media algorithms contribute to this by prioritizing engagement over accuracy. They show users content similar to what they've interacted with before, which reinforces their current viewpoints. The passage explains this creates confirmation bias. The consequences are serious - it leads to polarization where people become more extreme in their views, reduced exposure to diverse perspectives, and amplification of misinformation and sensational content. Basically, the algorithms trap people in bubbles where they only hear opinions they already agree with, which prevents them from understanding different viewpoints and can spread false information.

### Question 3 (10 points)
**Question:** The passage mentions that technology has "blurred the boundaries between work and personal life." Discuss what this means and explain the negative effects this can have on individuals.

**Your Answer:**
The blurred boundaries mean that the clear separation between work time and personal time has disappeared because of constant availability through mobile devices. People can be reached for work matters at any time, day or night, weekends or holidays. The passage specifically states this leads to increased stress and burnout. When you can never truly disconnect from work, your body and mind don't get proper rest. This constant state of being "on call" prevents people from fully engaging in personal activities, spending quality time with family, or pursuing hobbies. The mental health impact is significant because humans need clear boundaries to maintain psychological well-being and prevent exhaustion.

**Your Answer:**
First strategy: Redesign social media platforms to prioritize human well-being over engagement metrics, as the passage suggests. This could include showing users diverse perspectives intentionally, adding friction before sharing unverified information, and limiting addictive features like infinite scroll. Platforms could also be transparent about how their algorithms work so users understand what they're seeing and why.

Second strategy: Individuals should establish healthy boundaries with technology use, which the passage mentions. This could mean specific practices like no phones during meals, designated phone-free hours each day, turning off work notifications after certain hours, and having one day per week with minimal technology use. People could also practice intentional face-to-face communication by choosing in-person meetings over video calls when possible, to maintain those important communication skills the passage discusses.
